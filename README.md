## Water on Earth & Its Types

- **Earth’s Water Distribution:**
  - *96.5%* is salt water (oceans); not directly usable for drinking or agriculture.[10]
  - Only *1%* is easily accessible freshwater (mostly rivers and lakes); most is locked up in glaciers/icecaps (*68.7% of freshwater*) or as groundwater (*30.1% of freshwater*).[10]
  - The water cycle involves evaporation, condensation, precipitation (including rain and snow), and flows like runoff, transpiration, and infiltration.[10]

### Water Composition and Quality

- **Rainwater:** Slight acidity (pH ~5.7); main ions are **Na⁺ (sodium)** and **Cl⁻ (chloride)**.[10]
- **River Water:** Generally softer, lower in Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺; composition influenced by rain, erosion, rock weathering.[10]
- **Sea Water:** Higher pH (~8.1), full of **Na⁺**, **Cl⁻**, **Mg²⁺**, higher total dissolved solids (TDS).[10]
- **Groundwater:** pH ~7.4, high in Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺, more minerals, lower dissolved oxygen.[10]

## Water Pollution and Quality Testing

### Physical, Chemical, and Biological Parameters

- **Physical:** Temperature, color, turbidity (cloudiness).[10]
- **Chemical:** COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand), BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand), DO (Dissolved Oxygen), TDS, hardness, alkalinity, pH.[10]
- **Biological:** Viruses, bacteria, and pathogens—determine if water is safe.[10]

#### Key Water Quality Terms

- **BOD:** Oxygen needed by bacteria to break down organic matter over 5 days at 20°C (good: <2 mg/L for drinking, <30 for surface water).[10]
- **COD:** Oxygen equivalent needed to chemically oxidize organics (ideal: <250 mg/L).[10]
- **DO:** Dissolved oxygen; healthy water has 7–9 mg/L, fish can't survive <5 mg/L.[10]
- **Alkalinity:** Capacity to neutralize acids, mainly due to HCO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, OH⁻; gives water “buffering” ability.[10]
- **Hardness:** Due to Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺; makes soap hard to lather, causes scale.[10]
- **TDS (Total Dissolved Solids):** All chemicals dissolved in water; high TDS = salty/bitter taste.[10]

## Wastewater Treatment: Step-By-Step

**The goal** is to remove harmful solids, organics, and pathogens before returning water to nature or reusing it.

### 1. Preliminary Treatment

- **Purpose:** Remove large objects (rags, sticks, plastics) and grit (sand, gravel) that could damage equipment.[3][6]
- **How:** Screens and grit chambers.[2][3]

### 2. Primary Treatment

- **Purpose:** Remove suspended solids that settle by gravity.[5][6]
- **How:** Sedimentation tanks let solids (sludge) sink; oils/grease float and are skimmed.[6][7]
- **Effectiveness:** Removes 50–70% suspended solids but little dissolved organic matter.[5][6]

### 3. Secondary Treatment (Biological)

- **Purpose:** Remove dissolved organic matter and nutrients (like N, P).[7][3]
- **How:** Microorganisms (bacteria) in tanks consume organics. Common processes:
  - *Activated sludge*: Air is pumped in to help bacteria.[3][6]
  - *Trickling filter*: Wastewater passes over rocks/media coated in bacteria.[6]
- **Settling:** After treatment, bacteria clump together and settle out (secondary clarifier), forming additional sludge.[7][3]
- **Effectiveness:** Can remove >90% of organic matter; effluent is much cleaner.[2]

### 4. Tertiary Treatment (Advanced)

- **Purpose:** Remove remaining impurities, especially pathogens; achieve water reuse/discharge standards.[3][6]
- **How:** 
  - Filtration (sand, membrane)
  - Disinfection (chlorine, ozone, UV light)
  - Nutrient removal (nitrogen, phosphorus).[6][7]
- *Result*: Water is nearly as clean as drinking water (depends on standards needed).[3][6]

### 5. Sludge Treatment

- **Sludge** (solids collected in primary and secondary steps) is further treated:
  - *Thickened* to remove water
  - *Digested* (often anaerobically) to reduce pathogens and odors and generate biogas (methane).[1][7]
  - *Disposed* (landfill, compost, agricultural use).[1][6]

## Air Pollution: Basics & Control

- **Pollutants:** SOx (sulfur oxides), NOx (nitrogen oxides), CO, O₃, PM10, PM2.5 (fine particulates).[10]
- **Sources:** Vehicles, industry, burning waste, natural causes.[10]
- **Effects:** Respiratory diseases, visibility reduction, ecosystem harm.[10]
- **Control Methods:** Filters (HEPA for PM), electrostatic precipitators, catalytic converters, adsorption for gases.[10]
- **Measurement:** By mass concentration (e.g., micrograms of PM2.5 per m³ air), with sensors and filters.[10]

***

## Solid Waste Management

### Types

- *Municipal Solid Waste*: Everyday household waste
- *Hazardous*: Chemicals, batteries, e-waste.[10]

### Processing Steps

- **Collection:** From homes or institutions (primary); transfer to main stations (secondary).[10]
- **Separation:** Manual or mechanical sorting
- **Reduction:** Compaction (increase density), shredding (decrease size).[10]
- **Treatment:** Composting (biologically breaks down organics), anaerobic digestion (produces biogas), incineration (waste-to-energy).[10]
- **Landfill:** Final, controlled disposal with liners and leachate management.[10]

***

## Environmental Management & Laws

- **EMS (Environmental Management System):** Systematic way for organizations to track, improve, and show their environmental performance (ex: ISO 14001 standard).[10]
- **Indian Laws:** Water Act (1974), Air Act (1981), Environment Protection Act (1986).[10]
- **CPCB/NAAQS:** Set water/air quality standards.[10]

***

## Resource Recovery & Sustainability

- **Circular Economy:** Focus on reusing and recovering resources (energy from waste, nutrient recovery, wastewater reuse).[10]
- **Technologies:** Anaerobic digestion for energy, compost for fertilizer, membrane processes for water reuse.[10]
